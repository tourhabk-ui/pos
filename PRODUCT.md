# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Russian-speaking independent tourists and group travellers planning trips to Kamchatka. Split into two primary segments:

**Pre-trip planner (primary):** A person who has decided to go to Kamchatka — or is close to deciding — and needs to understand routes, danger, gear, accommodation, and how to get there. Often stressed about safety and logistics, not tech-savvy enough for specialist gear sites. May be planning 6–18 months out.

**On-trail user (critical):** The same person, now offline or on spotty LTE, following a route in real conditions. Needs GPS waypoints, SOS access, emergency contacts, and offline maps — instantly, without friction. This scenario is life-safety-relevant.

Secondary users: tour operators and guides managing bookings and availability through the /hub.

## Product Purpose

Vedar (TourHab / Volcano OS) is the single platform for Kamchatka travel. It replaces the fragmented stack of spreadsheets, Telegram chats, tour agency calls, and paper maps that tourists currently use. Success looks like: a tourist books a certified guide, registers with МЧС, downloads offline maps, and completes the route safely — all without leaving the platform.

The platform earns trust by being accurate and honest about danger, not by overselling the destination.

## Brand Personality

Wild · Honest · Bold

- **Wild**: rooted in Kamchatka's specific landscape — volcanoes, bears, geysers, thermal rivers, tundra. Not generic wilderness. The interface has the confidence of someone who has stood on Авачинский.
- **Honest**: states risk plainly. If a trail is dangerous in June, it says so. No marketing softening. Safety is the product.
- **Bold**: makes decisions. Does not hedge with infinite options. Recommends. Guides. Takes the user somewhere specific.

Tone: direct, earned authority. Not a corporate travel brochure. Not an Instagram adventure blog. The voice of an experienced local guide who respects the landscape and the person asking.

## Anti-references

- **Booking.com / Airbnb**: faceless aggregator with identical card templates, zero regional character, zero safety awareness. Vedar is place-specific, not platform-generic.
- **Советский турбюро**: bureaucratic, form-heavy, officious. Treats the tourist as an administrative unit. Vedar treats them as an adult making a real decision.
- **Startup-purple SaaS**: gradient-heavy, glassmorphism, "seamless" copy, tech-for-tech's-sake. Vedar's sophistication is in its data and safety logic, not its visual effects.
- **Instagram travel aesthetics**: pastel filters, lifestyle photography, influencer cadence. Vedar deals in facts, not moods.

## Design Principles

1. **Safety is information, not a disclaimer.** Danger appears at the point of decision — on the place card, on the route, in the planner — not buried in a FAQ. Design surfaces risk as a first-class data type.
2. **One recommendation beats ten options.** When the system knows enough to suggest, it suggests. Avoid UX that dumps all options and asks the user to choose. Kуzmich recommends; the planner pre-selects; the route page tells you what to pack.
3. **Offline is a feature, not an edge case.** Any screen a user might need on a trail must work without network. Maps, SOS, waypoints, gear list.
4. **Regional specificity over generic craft.** A volcano is not a mountain. A thermal river is not a hot spring. Every label, every category, every icon earns its place by being accurate to Kamchatka, not to a global travel template.
5. **Trust through honesty.** If accommodation data is incomplete, say so. If a route geometry is missing, show that clearly. Do not fake completeness. The platform's credibility is its most valuable asset.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. All interactive elements keyboard-navigable. Contrast ratios ≥4.5:1 for body text, ≥3:1 for large text. Reduced-motion alternative for all animations. SOS flow must be operable with one hand and gloves. Russian is the primary language; no assumption of English literacy.
